Game development, University of Michigan

A Grimoire's Tale

A top-down deck-building game in which a librarian collects spell books and combines their effects to save the library from an evil wizard. Nine spells, six enemy types and four bosses.

Built from scratch in seven weeks by a team of five for EECS 494, Game Design and Development, at the University of Michigan. Each week's build was playtested by classmates and course staff, and the recorded feedback set the next week's goals. The first theme, a modular robot, tested poorly, which led to the library setting; later rounds added a minimap and health and mana displays when players said they lacked information.

Role
Primary level designer; minimap, card selection and deck interfaces; enemy code and sound effects
Team
Toasty Lion Studios: Justin Kirsh, Robert Marzec, Alex Rivera, Wen-Chien Wang and Nithin Weerasinghe
Process
Weekly builds, playtest-driven iteration

Machine learning, coursework

Handwritten digit recognition

Digit classifiers trained on the MNIST dataset, implemented and compared across PyTorch, TensorFlow and Julia, then deployed as a web app where anyone could upload an image and get a prediction.

The pipeline ran on AWS from a Jupyter notebook packaged with Docker and served through Heroku. Because the computation ran on a professor's AWS account, access was password protected so that heavy traffic could not run up costs. A short video shows the app in use.

Models
PyTorch, TensorFlow, Julia
Deployment
Docker, AWS, Jupyter, Heroku

Music, University of Michigan

Refrain of Memory, arranged for carillon

An arrangement written during carillon lessons at the University of Michigan. The carillon is a keyboard instrument of at least 23 tuned bells, played with wooden batons for the hands and pedals for the feet.

The piece is "Refrain of Memory" by Kow Otani, from the soundtrack of Haibane Renmei. The arrangement is derived from Eric Wastl's piano arrangement, shared on the fan site Yatpay's Haibane Shrine, and adapted to the instrument:

  • The bass is simplified, since low notes are played with the feet.
  • Heavier bells need more force, so the bass carries less detail than the melody.
  • Chords are thinned to the number of notes the baton keyboard allows at once.
